# Adventure Template
This folder is for files that make up the current adventure being ran.
The template files in here use the SWAD framework for an adventure.

# SWAD
SWAD stands for Settlement, Wilds, and Dungeon. It is a framework for making fantasy TTRPG adventures that involve open world exploration and survival resource management. SWAD adventures take place in a single region that you define, and the region is made up of 3 main stages / locations the PCs can be in: the settlement, the wilds, and the dungeon.

The settlement is the main safe haven for the PCs. It's where PCs can safely rest, interact with NPCs that can help them, go shopping, buy services, do research, etc. Settlements are the stage that SWAD adventures typically start in. Settlements can have a few dangerous encounters to make them more interesting, but they should have a very low risk of the PCs dying in them to create contrast to the danger of the rest of the adventure. DMs are encouraged to use the [Downtime Rules](../Homebrew/Rules/Downtime.md) so players can have more options of things to do in settlements during and in between adventures.

The wilds are the locations that the PCs travel through to get to the dungeon and back to the settlement. This stage should be moderately dangerous, force the players to occassionally deal with forces of nature like weather, have lots of strange locations and NPCs to encounter, involve random combat encounters, and have helpful or thrilling secrets the players can uncover to reward them for exploring. Some of the random combat encounters can be prevented from happening in the future by searching for and destroying a source of enemies, such as a hideout, nest, or camp. PCs are typically meant to spend at least a few days in the wilds when traveling between the dungeon and the settlement to make them use more of their resources, which makes the journey to and back from the dungeon more thrilling, engaging, and dangerous. PCs also typically don't know exactly where the dungeon is at the start of the adventure, causing them to spend more time in the wilds until they find the dungeon. SWAD adventures can also sometimes start in the wilds. DMs are encouraged to use the [Survival Mechanics Rules](../Homebrew/Rules/Survival%20Mechanics.md) to give players structured and engaging rules for collecting food, water, and crafting ingredients in the wilds, as well as to make poison a more used mechanic.

The dungeon is the focal point of the adventure where the PCs' main goal lies. It is the most dangerous stage, being full of dangerous encounters that drain the players' resources. Dungeons in SWAD adventures should be huge with many encounters, like a mega dunegon, to force PCs to take multiple trips in and out of the dungeon before they complete their objective. It should also have enough encounters to force the players to need to take multiple trips back and forth through the wilds and back to the settlement to restock on supplies, sell treasure, and research more about the dungeon. Dungeons should be at least a few days of travel through the wilds away from the settlement to make the journey to them and back from them more tense and interesting. The exact location of dungeons is also typically not known to the PCs at the start of SWAD adventures, forcing the players to explore through the wilds for a while to find an entrance to the dungeon. DMs are encouraged to use the [Alt Rest Healing Rules](../Homebrew/Rules/Alt%20Rest%20Healing%20Rules.md) to make players put more effort into managing their resources, and to make healing from resting more realistic.

These 3 stages should create a gameplay loop of trekking through the wilds, spending a few days camping outside the dungeon going in and out of it, treking back to the settlement, and repeating. It should:

- Give the adventure an open world feeling from the exploration options
- Make the PCs feel a connection to the locations in the various stages due spending lots of time in them
- Have highs and lows of excitment for pacing when going in and out of the settlement
- Make the players spend more effort into managing their resources
- Feel fresh due to the large number of unique encounters and occassional random encounters

To get help with determining XP rewards for non-combat encounters, use the [Non-Combat Encounter XP Rules](../Homebrew/Rules/Non-Combat%20Encounter%20XP.md).

# Steps to Making SWAD Adventure with This Template
1. Think of the outline of the adventure.
	- Adventure.md
2. Come up with a hook, settlement, wilds, dungeon, and epilogue for the adventure.
	- Adventure.md
3. Write the description and environment of the settlement, wilds, and dungeon.
	- Settlement.md
	- Wilds.md
	- Dungeon.md
4. Come up with the navigation mechanics of the wilds.
	- Wilds.md
5. Create random combat encounters for the wilds and the dungeon.
	- Wilds.md
	- Dungeon.md
6. Come up with all of the locations and encounters for the settlement, wilds, and dungeon.
	- Settlement.md
	- Wilds.md
	- Dungeon.md
7. Fill out the details for each location and encounter.
	- Settlement.md
	- Wilds.md
	- Dungeon.md
8. Make a map of the wilds, which includes the town and dungeon in it.
9. Make a map of the town.
10. Make a map of the dungeon.
11. Make maps of each encounter where there might be combat.
12. Review each part of the entire adventure and make revisions.
	- Adventure.md
	- Settlement.md
	- Wilds.md
	- Dungeon.md
