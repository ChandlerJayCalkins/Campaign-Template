# Survival Mechanics
These rules define how players are able to obtain things that they need in the environment, like safe / comfortable shelter, food, clean water, potion ingredients, etc. It also uses poison mechanics to introduce an extra challenge to surviving in the wild and to make poison a more common mechanic.

## Food & Ingredients
***Foraging.*** A Search action is taken in the environment for 1 hour, Wisdom (Survival) DC determined by Flora Foraging Results table in the Environment section of a stage.

The result of this ability check determines which flora the character taking this action encounters and how much of each kind of flora they encounter. The character chooses how much of each flora they harvest up to the maximum amount they encounter, but they must have a way to carry it all.

To determine how much of each flora a character encounters, compare their foraging ability check result to the Flora Foraging Results table in the Environment section of the stage. This table has 3 tiers for each type of flora. The higher the tier, the more of that flora the character encounters, but it also has a higher DC to reach that tier. For each type of flora, the character encounters an amount equal to the highest tier DC that their foraging ability check result succeeded against. If a character's foraging result does not meet the tier 1 DC of a flora, then that character does not encounter any of that type of flora. If a character's foraging result does not meet the tier 1 DC of any flora, then that character does not encounter any of the listed types of flora in the table.

For example, if a character gets a 12 on their foraging ability check, using the Flora Foraging Results table from the Wilds template in the Adventure Template, that character would encounter 1d4 + 4 oz of Brush Berry since their foraging check result reached tier 2 for that flora, as well as 1 piece of Nizzletip since their check result reached tier 1 for that flora.

If a PC encounters a flora they've never encountered before or are not otherwise familiar with, describe the flora to that player. The PC can then take a Study action at the flora to identify. If they do so, they must make an Intelligence (Nature) ability check. The DC for successfully identifying the flora is in the Flora Information table in the Environment section of a stage. If the PC succeeds the ability check, they get the flora's name and its full description.

***Hunting.*** A Search action is taken in the environment for 1 hour, Wisdom (Perception) DC determined by Fauna Tracking table in the environment section of a stage.

The result of this ability check determines what fauna signs the character taking this action encounters. A fauna sign is evidence of a certain fauna in the area, such as footprints, feathers / fur, droppings, scratch marks, etc. A character can choose whether or not to pursue a certain fauna sign that they encounter. If a character encounters multiple fauna signs, they can only choose one to pursue. If a character encounters a fauna sign they've never encountered before or are not otherwise familiar with, describe the fauna sign to that player. The PC can then take a Study action at the fauna sign to identify what fauna it's from. If they do so, they must make an Intelligence (Nature) ability check. The DC for successfully identifying what fauna its from is in the Fauna Tracking table. If the PC succeeds the ability check, they get told what fauna that's a sign of.

To determine what fauna signs a character encounters, compare their tracking ability check result to the Fauna Tracking table in the Environment section of the stage. The character encounters fauna signs of any fauna their check result succeeds against the Tracking DC of that fauna in the Fauna Tracking table. If the character chooses to pursue a fauna sign, they must then take another Search action for an amount of time specified by that fauna's pursuit time in the Fauna Tracking table. At the end of that specified time, the character makes a Wisdom (Survival) ability check to locate the fauna that left the sign. If the character has not identified what fauna the sign came from, they make this check with disadvantage. The DC for this check is determined by the Tracking DC of that fauna.

If the character fails this ability check, they are unable to locate the fauna and must look for new fauna sign of that fauna to attempt pursuing it again. If the character succeeds, they locate the fauna. To capture or kill the fauna, they must take the Hide action and make a Dexterity (Stealth) ability check to get close enough to the fauna to attack it. The DC for this ability check is the fauna's Passive Perception. If the character successfully hides from the fauna, they get within normal range of their attack from the fauna. If the character fails to sneak up to the fauna or it escape from their attack, the fauna runs away and a chase can ensue of the character chooses to chase it. If the character loses the fauna in the chase, they restart the process of hunting to hunt that fauna again.

If a character successfully kills or captures a fauna, the Fauna Tracking table lists what can be harvested from it. When consumable items are harvested from the fauna, roll a d100 and use the corresponding chance of poison for that fauna to determine if the consumable items from it are poison.

## Water
***Finding a Water Source.*** Search action in the environment, Wisdom (Survival) DC determined by the Environment section of a stage.
- *Success.* A water source is successfully found after some number of hours determined by the Environment section of a stage. Roll the specified die from the Water Source Type table in the Environment section for that stage to determine the type of water source found. Then, whenever a creature collects water from that water source, roll a d100 and use the corresponding chance of poison for that water source to determine if the water they collected is poison.
- *Failure.* After 6 hours of searching, no water source is found.

***Collecting Rain Water.*** Various pieces of equipment can be used to collect rainwater. The amount of rainwater that can be collected by a piece of equipment in a certain amount of time is determined by the level of rainfall occurring (moderate or heavy) and a die roll based on the piece of equipment in the Rainwater Collection Rates table below. If rainwater is being collected with a piece of equipment not listed on this table, you can use a similar piece of equipment on the table to determine how much rainwater it collects. Equipment that has an opening with a larger surface area collects rainwater at a faster rate. Equipment can only be used to collect rainwater if it is made of a watertight material, otherwise the water leaks out and can't be held in.

#### Rainwater Collection Rates
| Equipment | Moderate Rainfall | Heavy Rainfall |
|-|-|-|
| Barrel | 1 gallon per 1d6 hours | 2d12 gallons per hour |
| Chest | 1d3 gallons per hour | 1d10 x 3 gallons per hour |
| Waterskin | Full in 1d12 hours | Full in 1d6 x 10 minutes |
| Backpack, Bucket, Iron Pot, Jug | 1 gallon per 1d12 hours | 2d6 gallons per hour |
| Case, Flask, Glass Bottle, Pouch, Quiver, Sack, Vial | Full in 1d6 x 10 minutes | Full in 1d10 minutes |

## Poison Food & Water
***Detecting if Flora is Poison.*** Identifying the flora with a Study action and a successful Intelligence (Nature) ability check will give a description of whether a flora is poisonous or not.

***Detecting if Meat is Poison.*** Search action at the meat, Wisdom (Medicine) DC 18.
- *Success.* Can tell if the meat is poison or not.
- *Failure.* Cannot see any evidence of the meat being poison.

***Finding a Clean Spot to Collect Water.*** Search action at a water source, Wisdom (Survival) DC 16.
- *Success.* Find a safe spot to collect clean water from the water source. Chance of water being poison when collecting from that spot is lowered by half.
- *Failure.* Cannot tell where a safe spot to collect clean water from the water source is.

***Consuming Poison Food or Water.*** Creatures that have consumed poison food or water must make a DC 13 Constitution Saving Throw upon the morning of the next day (typically upon finishing a Long Rest).
- *Success.* No effect.
- *Failure.* The creature has the Poisoned condition. Each time the creature takes a Long Rest, they can make this Saving Throw again to end the Poisoned condition. If the creature consumed poison food or water again between Saving Throws to end the condition, they make the next Saving Throw with Disadvantage.
